## Design Specificity Verdict

The architecture is specific to TGC, but the current deep-page narratives are not yet specific enough in form. Hardware barriers, cloud delivery, AI optimisation, cloud agnosticism, device access, and network adaptation recur across Home, Platform, Technology, and Company. If implemented directly, the site could feel like one pitch deck split across routes rather than a purpose-built corporate narrative.

The reference sites earn long pages through distinct evidence and varied editorial chapters. Vantra uses short copy and project/status proof. Metacci uses cases, process, metrics, and FAQ. Room & Pillar concentrates length in portfolio evidence and two substantial biographies. TGC currently uses repeated abstract proposition copy where those sites use proof or a new kind of content.

The deterministic detector returned no findings for `content/pages`. This is not a clean bill of health: the detector does not provide meaningful density analysis for Markdown planning files. Direct structural and word-count analysis was used instead.

## Overall Impression

The six-page split is strong and the governance discipline is unusually good. The main opportunity is editorial compression: make every route reveal something genuinely new, attach proof closer to claims, and let visuals replace repeated explanation.

## What Is Working

1. **Progressive page architecture.** Home introduces, Platform explains value, Technology provides engineering depth, Company establishes identity, Leadership supplies people proof, and Contact converts.
2. **Credibility controls.** Excluded slides, confidential material, unverifiable claims, and approval requirements are clearly documented.
3. **Strong narrative spine.** Hardware barrier, adaptive platform, technical method, evidence, people, and contact form a coherent corporate story.

## Priority Issues

### P1 - Platform and Technology overlap

**Why it matters:** Visitors receive increasingly technical versions of the same cloud-delivery explanation rather than a clear progression from value to method.

**Fix:** Platform owns audiences, outcomes, and one simple player-to-cloud journey. Technology owns system layers, four differentiators, test methodology, and engineering-reviewed answers.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### P1 - Deep pages contain too many equal-weight chapters

**Why it matters:** Nine or ten chapters per page creates pitch-deck pacing, especially on mobile, and leaves insufficient room for the dramatic scale changes present in the references.

**Fix:** Reduce Platform and Technology to 6-7 visitor chapters and Company to 5-6. Alternate statement, visual explanation, proof, and human context instead of repeating heading-paragraph-card grids.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### P1 - Proof is too late and detached from claims

**Why it matters:** Visitors must accept several propositions before encountering demonstrations, recognition, methodology, or verified company facts.

**Fix:** Add one early verified proof band on Home and attach technical evidence beside the exact Technology claim it supports.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### P2 - Internal planning content and public copy share one hierarchy

**Why it matters:** The six briefs contain 5,750 words, 23 verification labels, and 157 directive-keyword occurrences. A builder could accidentally render planning notes or preserve every proposed section.

**Fix:** Separate public copy, verification claims, editorial notes, visual direction, implementation requirements, exclusions, and metadata in the content model.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### P2 - CTA repetition may become mechanical

**Why it matters:** Contact is correctly persistent, but identical hero and close treatments on every route can flatten the journey.

**Fix:** Keep Contact persistent in navigation and use one strong close per page. Use contextual hero actions only where immediate intent supports them.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

### Jordan, first-time corporate visitor

- Repeated explanations make Platform and Technology difficult to distinguish.
- AI, cloud agnosticism, AV1, buffering, GPU utilisation, and input handling can arrive before a simple outcome is established.
- FAQ inventories without answers would create dead ends rather than reassurance.

### Casey, distracted mobile visitor

- Nine or ten chapters on a deep page creates excessive scroll length before new value appears.
- Biography plus four focus bullets makes each leadership profile too tall.
- Repeated CTAs and section links compete for attention instead of preserving one clear path.

### Riley, deliberate evaluator

- Proof is separated from the claims it is meant to substantiate.
- Verification placeholders and unsupported association logos would reduce trust if they reached production.
- Company lacks the founding narrative needed to explain why TGC, rather than only what it claims to build.

## Minor Observations

- Home should preview Technology with three short differentiators, not four mini-explanations.
- Leadership should use 45-65 words per person and remove repetitive focus lists.
- Contact is structurally sound; conditional states and backend requirements should remain implementation-only.
- Platform and Technology FAQs should not be public until approved answers exist.
- The Company page should not replace a missing founding story with more platform proposition copy.

## Questions to Consider

- What is the earliest verified proof TGC can place immediately after the Home hero?
- Which one idea belongs exclusively to Platform, and which one belongs exclusively to Technology?
- Can the Company team provide a short founding story before design begins?
- Which claims can be supported by official sources, test conditions, or approved partner evidence?
